Why things were built this way

Longer notes on the real decisions behind each initiative — added here as they're written up, so the main Vision page stays a quick read.

Online Storefront

Why Shopify, then Claude for Recipe Bot & FAQ Bot

Shopify was chosen for the commodity infrastructure it does well — storefront, inventory, payments, discounted shipping labels, fraud detection — and its own AI assistant, Sidekick, makes day-to-day site maintenance straightforward.

Recipe Bot and FAQ Bot were built with Claude Code rather than Shopify's own AI — a deliberate experiment to see how well AI logic built outside Shopify's ecosystem would hold up once integrated into a live storefront. Sidekick was then used for the integration itself: wiring the finished bots into the actual site rather than rebuilding that Shopify-specific layer from scratch.

Internal Operations Tool

Why we built it custom

The ops tool was built around one goal: staying flexible while Sivan Spices' actual pricing, invoicing, commission, and inventory needs are still evolving. Off-the-shelf options didn't fit — even a single $10/month Shopify invoicing app wasn't flexible enough, and covering everything the business actually needs (invoicing, inventory, commissions, leads, returns) would mean stitching together a different specialized tool for each one, none of which talk to each other.

Building custom instead — using AI-assisted development — meant the tool could change as fast as the business does: the pricing logic alone has already been rebuilt multiple times as real store agreements turned out more flexible than first assumed.

Looking ahead — future consideration for productization: most food startup founders are running a one-person show, with no time to build or manage their own tools — and most don't have a software background, so AI-assisted development isn't something they can lean on the way this was built. That's a real gap: the same approach that solved this problem for Sivan Spices could help other early-stage food founders get simple, actionable tools of their own, without each of them needing an engineering background to get there. Not something in motion yet — just a direction worth noting.

Sivan Cooks

A goal/loop-driven architecture, built solo rather than hired out

Sivan Cooks was a chance to build a real, goal/loop-driven architecture from scratch — a genuinely interesting technical problem on its own, not just a business one. Hiring designers and developers to build this out felt like overkill this early: the shape of the problem itself was still being figured out, which called for something one person could iterate on quickly with Claude Code, not a team executing against a fixed spec.

Where a cook/client enters Claude-powered Supabase (source of truth) Rule-based, not AI Scheduled cron job, not AI

Reading it: not everything here is AI, on purpose. Entry parsing, scoring, and coaching are genuinely Claude-powered. Verification is a deliberately simple rule (auto-approve the first N, everyone else to manual review) — no automated ID/background-check service. Reminders and dormancy are plain scheduled jobs. All of it reads from and writes back to one Supabase source of truth rather than separate systems bolted together, which is the "loop" part: scoring and coaching re-run as new signal — a rating, a status change — comes in, not as one-time gates.
